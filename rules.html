Rules

How scores and rankings on this site are calculated.

Individual contest ranking

Within a single contest

Each contest is scored the standard ICPC way, exactly as Codeforces reports it. Contestants are ordered by problems solved, most first. Ties are broken by penalty, lowest first.

A solved problem contributes its solve time in minutes, plus 20 minutes for every rejected submission on that problem. Rejected submissions on problems you never solve cost nothing. So solving a problem at 01:29 after one wrong answer contributes 89 + 20 = 109.

Across all contests

The overall ranking on the Individual Contests page is average placement: add up your finishing positions and divide by the number of contests you entered. Lower is better. Currently 1 contest counts toward it.

Only contests you actually entered count. Missing a round does not add a bad score to your average — it simply is not counted.

Tie-breaks

When two people have the same average placement, in order:

  1. More contests played wins. An average over three rounds is better evidence than the same average over one.
  2. Better single finish wins.
  3. Name, alphabetically. This is arbitrary and only exists so the order is stable.

People who have not competed yet

Roster members who have not appeared in any scoreboard are listed separately under Yet to compete, not ranked at the bottom. Someone who has not competed has no measured performance, and giving them a placeholder score would misrepresent them.

Why average placement

Placement is comparable across contests in a way that raw scores are not: solving 3 of 6 hard problems and 5 of 6 easy ones can be the same achievement, but the totals look nothing alike. Averaging positions normalises for how hard a given problemset happened to be.

The trade-off is that it rewards consistency over peaks, and an average over a single contest is not yet meaningful. That is why contests played is shown next to every average.
